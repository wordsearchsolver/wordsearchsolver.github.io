Pro Advice

Tips for Anagram Hunting

Improve your anagram skills and get the highest scores in your word games.

1

Look for Common Suffixes

Words ending in -ING, -ED, -ER, -LY, -TION are everywhere. If you see those letters in your rack, try forming words with them and see what else sticks.

2

Start With Vowels

Vowels are usually the fewest letters in your rack, so start there. Knowing which vowels you have limits the possible words dramatically.

3

Use All Letters When Possible

In Scrabble, using all 7 of your letters in one turn earns a bonus of 50 points (a "bingo"). Try the Exact Anagrams mode first to look for 7-letter words.

4

Don't Forget 2-Letter Words

In Scrabble, 2-letter words are often the key to scoring well because they let you hook onto existing words. Memorize the 2-letter word list!

5

Sort by Length for High Scores

When playing Scrabble, use the "Longest First" sort. Longer words generally score more points, especially if they hit bonus squares.

6

Sub-Anagrams Save the Day

Sometimes you can't use all your letters. Switch to Sub-Anagram mode to find smaller words that still score well or set up your next move.

Background

What Is an Anagram?

An anagram is a word or phrase formed by rearranging the letters of another word or phrase. For example, "LISTEN" is an anagram of "SILENT" — both use the exact same six letters.

Anagrams have been used in wordplay for centuries. They appear in literature, puzzles, and games, and are famously used in cryptic crossword clues (often signaled by words like "arranged," "confused," "broken," or "mixed").

There are two main types: exact anagrams (using every letter) and partial or sub-anagrams (using a subset of letters). Both are valuable when solving word puzzles or playing games like Scrabble and Words With Friends.

Our Anagram Solver uses letter-frequency counting to quickly scan a large word database and surface every valid anagram and sub-anagram. It's the fastest way to unscramble letters and find hidden words.
