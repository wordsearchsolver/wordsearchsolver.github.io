🎓 Minesweeper Strategy Tips

  • Start with the corners and edges. Fewer neighbours means numbers give more precise information.
  • A 1 touching one hidden square. If a 1 has just one unrevealed neighbour, that square is a mine.
  • Count flags against numbers. When a number’s flags are all placed, every other neighbour is safe, so you can open them in one tap by chording.
  • Look for 1-2-1 patterns. In a row of 1-2-1 along a wall, the mines usually sit under the two outer squares of the three beneath.
  • Guess last, and guess smart. If you must guess, pick the square with the lowest chance of a mine rather than a random one.

About Minesweeper

Minesweeper is a logic puzzle that has been shipped with Windows for decades, which is why so many people know it. The board is a grid of covered squares, some hiding mines. Revealing a square shows a number that counts the mines in the eight squares around it, or opens a region if there are none.

It is a game of deduction rather than luck once the first move is made. Each number is a constraint, and by combining constraints from neighbouring squares you can often prove that a square is safe or mined. Higher difficulties simply use a larger board with a higher density of mines.
