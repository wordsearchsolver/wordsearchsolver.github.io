Pro Advice

Tips for Great Word Search Puzzles

Get the best results from the generator and create puzzles people love to solve.

1

Use 10–15 Words for a 10×10 Grid

Too many words make the grid crowded and hard to read. Too few make it too easy. As a rule of thumb, use about one word for every 7–10 cells of grid area.

2

Mix Word Lengths

Combine short words (3–4 letters) with longer ones (6–10 letters) for a better puzzle. Long words fill more space and make the puzzle more interesting.

3

Choose Themed Words

Themed puzzles are more fun and educational. Use words related to a holiday, science topic, book, or event. The built-in word list has plenty of options to get you started.

4

Test the Puzzle First

After generating, print a copy and solve it yourself to make sure it's not too easy or too hard. You can regenerate with different settings if needed.

5

Use Hard Mode Sparingly

Hard mode includes reverse and diagonal placements, which can make puzzles frustrating for young kids. Reserve it for adult puzzle fans or advanced students.

6

Print on Thick Paper

For classroom use or puzzle books, print on thicker paper so erasing doesn't tear the page. Use a pencil rather than a pen when solving.

Background

What Is a Word Search Puzzle?

A word search puzzle (also called a word find, word seek, or word sleuth) is a word game where letters are arranged in a rectangular grid. A list of hidden words accompanies the grid, and the goal is to locate each word within the jumble of letters.

Words can be hidden horizontally, vertically, or diagonally, and may read forwards or backwards. The remaining letter spaces are filled with random letters to disguise the hidden words.

Word searches have been a staple of educational and recreational puzzles since the late 1960s. They are used in classrooms to reinforce vocabulary, in therapy to improve cognitive engagement, and in newspapers and magazines as casual entertainment.

Our Word Search Generator automates the puzzle creation process. Enter your words, choose your settings, and get a complete puzzle ready to print — no manual grid design needed.
